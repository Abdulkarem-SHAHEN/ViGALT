\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We collected 30 product images from Amazon.fr (15 clothing, 15 furniture) covering children's, sports, and women's garments with prints, logos, and characters, and beds, tables, mirrors, and sofas of varied shape, material, and staging. We ran the public \base code \cite{mohanbabu2024} on the same images, replacing its deprecated GPT-4V backend with \gen, the model \sys uses. Differences can thus be attributed to pipeline design rather than model capability. Each final text from both systems is decomposed into atomic claims and judged by a stronger, identical judge (\judge). Scores are computed per image and macro-averaged, so each product counts equally.

\textit{Metrics.} For an image with $N$ atomic claims, the judge labels each claim; the per-image scores are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Obj} &= N_{\mathrm{obj}}/N, \qquad \mathrm{Rel} = 100\cdot\frac{N_{\mathrm{rel}}+0.5N_{\mathrm{just}}}{N_{\mathrm{rel}}+N_{\mathrm{just}}+2N_{\mathrm{viol}}}, \label{eq:objrel}\\
\mathrm{RN} &= N_{\mathrm{nov}\wedge\mathrm{rel}}/N, \qquad \mathrm{AR} = N_{\mathrm{avoid}}/N, \label{eq:nov}\\
\mathrm{Eff} &= 100\cdot N_{\mathrm{nov}\wedge\mathrm{rel}}/W, \label{eq:eff}
\end{align}
where $N_{\mathrm{obj}}$ counts observable (non-subjective, non-marketing) claims; $N_{\mathrm{rel}}$, $N_{\mathrm{just}}$, and $N_{\mathrm{viol}}$ count on-product relevant (\texttt{R}), justified staging (\texttt{S}), and off-focus violation (\texttt{V}) claims assigned by the relevancy judge (same rubric as our automated evaluator); $N_{\mathrm{nov}\wedge\mathrm{rel}}$ counts claims that are \texttt{NOVEL} with respect to the DOM \emph{and} relevant; $N_{\mathrm{avoid}}$ counts \texttt{AVOIDABLE\_REDUNDANCY} claims; and $W$ is the word count of the alt text. Relevancy uses fixed weights $w=0.5$ on staging and $\lambda=2$ on violations, so off-focus detail is penalized more strongly than in a plain relevant-claim fraction; clothing listings have $N_{\mathrm{just}}=0$ by rubric. Per-image $\mathrm{Rel}$ is reported on a 0--100 scale; tables and figures macro-average $\mathrm{Rel}/100$ over the 30 products. Relevant Novelty (RN) prevents irrelevant scene detail from counting as useful novelty, and Information Efficiency (Eff) rewards concision, following W3C guidance \cite{w3ctips}. Lower is better only for Avoidable Redundancy (AR). Paired differences are tested with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test \cite{wilcoxon1945}, and $p$-values are Holm-corrected across the five metrics \cite{holm1979}.
